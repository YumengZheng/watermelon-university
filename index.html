STEP 1

Field Spot

Find the field spot
the darker the better

Why?

BAD

OK

GOOD

When you are picking a watermelon, look for the field spot. The field spot is where the watermelon sat on the ground and ripened in the sun. The darker the field spot is, the longer the watermelon was getting sunlights. If the field spot is white, or even nonexistent, this probably means that the watermelon was picked too soon, and will not be ripe.

STEP 2

Webbings

The more the better

Why?

BAD

OK

GOOD

Webbings are the weblike spots on the watermelon. It means that bees touched the pollinating parts of the watermelon in its early stages. The more pollination, the sweeter the fruit is. Trust the bees; they know where the sweetness is!

STEP 3

Weight

The heavier the better

Why?

BAD

OK

GOOD

Good watermelons are heavy for their size, because heavier weights are indicative of higher water content (and thus greater ripeness). If you compare the weight of one watermelon with another of equal size - the heavier one will be the riper one.

A dried tail indicates that the watermelon is ripe. However, if the tail is green, it probably means that the watermelon was picked too soon and will not be ripe.
